Wire Fraud Protection

Never take wire instructions from an email

Wire fraud is the most expensive thing that can happen in a real estate transaction, and it is not a technical attack. It is a well-written email that arrives at exactly the right moment in your escrow, from an address that looks almost right.

This is what one looks like

Below is a reconstruction of a real pattern. Four things in it give it away. See how many you find before you read the notes.

The people who send these have usually been reading the transaction for weeks, from a compromised mailbox somewhere in the deal. That is why the timing is right and the details are correct.

Spot the fake — four tells

0 of 4 found

From
Subject

Good news — the lender can record early, so we are moving your closing forward. Your funds need to arrive or the rate lock expires.

Please note that . Use the account details below and disregard the instructions in your opening package.

Reply to this message to confirm receipt. — Escrow Department

  • The domain is one letter off. wonderlandescrows.com is not us. Lookalike domains are registered specifically for your transaction.
  • Urgency is the whole technique. A real closing date change reaches you from your officer by phone, not as a subject line demanding same-day money.
  • A deadline you cannot verify. Nothing in escrow requires you to wire within hours. If it did, your officer would call you.
  • Wiring instructions never change. This is the one that costs people their down payment. Our banking details are set when the file opens and stay put.

That is all four. The rule that beats every one of them: we never send new wire instructions by email. If anything about a wire looks different, stop and call your officer on the number from your opening package — never a number in the email.

Our Side Of It

What we do about it

None of this is optional or situational. It is how every file runs, on every transaction, at every office.

  • Verbal verification of every wire, every time, on a number we already hold for you
  • We never change wire instructions mid-transaction — if an email says we did, it did not come from us
  • Encrypted document delivery rather than email attachments
  • Same-business-day confirmation once your funds land
  • Banking details are set when the file opens and stay put until it closes

If Something Looks Wrong

Stop, and pick up the phone

If you have already sent funds and something feels off, the first hour matters more than anything else. Wires can sometimes be recalled if the banks move quickly.

  1. Do not reply to the email. If it is fraudulent, you are writing to the person who sent it.
  2. Call your escrow officer on the number in your opening package, or the office direct line on our locations page. Never a number from the email.
  3. Call your bank’s fraud line immediately and ask them to attempt a recall on the wire. Ask for a case number.
  4. Report it to the FBI at ic3.gov. Their Recovery Asset Team can work with the receiving bank, and they are far more effective the sooner they hear about it.

Not Sure About Something?

Ask us before you send anything

There is no such thing as a wire question that is too small. Your officer would far rather take the call.
